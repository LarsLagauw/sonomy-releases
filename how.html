Sonomy is built the other way around.

Every other notes app starts with a server. This one starts with your machine.

LocalPrivateDecentral

Scroll, or use the arrow keys.

Local.

Your notes are ordinary files in a folder you picked, and everything that reads them runs on the machine in front of you.

The model that hears you runs here.

A recording goes into a model sitting on your own disk and comes back as text, with the voices told apart. The sound never leaves the machine, and there is no bill for the minutes.

So does the one that understands you.

Every sentence you write is given a place among the others, worked out on your own processor. Search walks that space instead of matching letters, which is why a question in Dutch finds a note in English.

Private.

Nothing leaves this machine unless you send it. What you do send is locked before it goes, and only the person you sent it to can open it.

It stops making sense the moment it leaves.

Your machine scrambles the page against a key their machine holds, and only their machine can put it back. Everyone along the way is handed the middle picture.

In between, there is nothing to read.

Whatever carries it, a network, a cable, somebody else's computer, is handed a locked page and nothing else. Not the words, not the pictures, not the name of the page.

Decentral.

Your machines are the network. There is no middle to read you, to lose your work, or to switch off.

Your machines reach each other.

On the same desk or on another continent, they hand pages straight to one another and keep each other in step. Nothing sits in the middle holding a copy.

Nothing to rent. Nothing to bill.

Your machines do the work, so a thousand users cost the same as one. Everything Sonomy does today stays free.
